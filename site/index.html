Interface stability of sulfide solid electrolytes against lithium metal anodes

L. Chena, M. Okaforb, R. Satoa,*

a Department of Materials Science, Tohoku University · b Institute for Energy Storage, ETH Zürich

Abstract Sulfide electrolytes reach 10−2 S/cm at room temperature [12], yet interfacial decomposition against lithium metal limits cycle life. We combine impedance spectroscopy with operando XPS to resolve the reaction front [8], and show that a 5 nm LiNbO3 buffer suppresses interphase growth by 74 %.

Keywords: solid electrolyte · sulfide · interphase · operando XPS

1. Introduction

Solid-state batteries promise higher energy density and intrinsic safety, provided the electrolyte remains stable against both electrodes [1,2]. Among candidates, Li10GeP2S12 (LGPS) combines the highest bulk conductivity with the narrowest stability window.

In the bulk, ionic transport follows an Arrhenius relation between conductivity and temperature,

σ(T) = σ0 exp(−Ea / kBT)(1)

where Ea is the activation energy. Fig. 1 compares LGPS with Al-doped LLZO across 25–100 °C.

Abstract (structured)

Background. Micro- and nanoplastics are pervasive pollutants now detected in blood, faeces, urine, lung tissue and placenta [1–4]; whether internal burden is associated with systemic inflammation in humans is not established.

Objective. To identify and appraise human observational studies (2018–2026) relating a microplastic exposure proxy to inflammatory markers (CRP, IL-6, TNF-α, fibrinogen).

Results. In the largest study (n = 420), urinary MP concentration was associated with hs-CRP (β = 0.28, P < 0.001) and IL-6 (β = 0.13, P = 0.002) [7]. Carotid-plaque MNPs predicted a composite of MI, stroke or death (HR 4.53, 95 % CI 2.00–10.27) [9].

Conclusions. Internal MP burden is consistently but weakly associated with inflammatory markers — most reproducibly IL-6 — across urinary, blood and faecal matrices. The evidence base does not support causal claims.
